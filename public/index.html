New Study Shows How Young People Feel About AI Companions

Nov 19, 2026 03:30 | Source: slashdot.org

An anonymous reader quotes a report from 404 Media: A new study from Gallup looked at how young people across the world are making friends, both digitally and in person. The good news is that around 93% of the young people surveyed reported having at least one strong in-person friendship. The weird news is that 33% said they had an AI companion. Gallup's study is lengthy and nuanced. It partnered with the Boston Children's Digital Wellness Lab to conduct the study and used online surveys of more than 16,000 young people from the ages of 15 to 22 in 16 different countries. A lot of the topline results won't surprise anyone living in today's constantly online digital-forward age. Gallup found that respondents felt their in-person friendships were their strongest, that digital friendships complemented rather than replaced in-person ones, and that stronger friendships made people happier no matter how the connection was made. Three things that stood out were how the young respondents talked about AI, loneliness, and doomscrolling. Young people in the United States, Canada, and UK reported the least interest in AI overall, with only 20% saying they used it once a day or more. Almost 40 percent of young people in Brazil use it daily. Japan and Italy both come in around 35%. "Nearly half of young people in the United States, United Kingdom and Canada have used AI no more than a few times in the past month, including those who have not used it at all," the researchers wrote. Most of the respondents (82%) said they'd used AI to look up information, 68% asked it for advice, and 33% used it as a companion. Young people with online-only friends were more likely to use AI for companionship, "although these patterns do not establish whether AI companionship supplements or substitutes for human relationships," they wrote. The researchers discovered that people in different countries felt wildly different about their AI buddies. Almost half of Australians with AI friends rated the relationship as "high quality" and only 12% of young people in Poland did. Mostly, though, people thought of their AI friends as mid: "In most countries [...] young people are more likely to give their AI companionship a medium rating than either a high or low rating." People who used AI as a friend also reported higher levels of loneliness, the study found. "However, the findings cannot determine whether young people turn to AI because they feel lonely, whether AI companionship influences their social experiences or whether other circumstances help explain these relationships," the researchers wrote. [...] The study's authors warned about reading too much into these results. "These assessments should be compared cautiously with those for human friendships examined in this report, as young people may have different expectations of what makes an AI companion or human friendship meaningful or supportive," the researchers wrote. "The broader message is not that one form of connection is inherently better or worse, but that the quality of young people's relationships and the wider social context in which they exist matter greatly," the study concluded.
